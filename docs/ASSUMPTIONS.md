# DaluMaga assumptions log

All 35 entries are unverified. Risk ratings are provisional design judgments for the one-factory, one-route, one-vehicle pilot. High means an error could compromise weight records, custody, safety, staff privacy or a time-critical decision. Med means it could disrupt operations or mislead routine review. Low means it mainly affects usability. The fuel entry is for a later phase.

| DM-001 | ASSUMPTION — Pickup and factory staff can weigh the same intact crate before its contents are tipped out. | High — An individual shortage could be attributed to the wrong crate. | Ask the receiver to show an ordinary arrival by video, from unloading to tipping. Match the unit weighed to a receiving slip. | Factory receiver and receiving supervisor | Configure crate-level or consignment-level receipt. Compare only matching units and mark any missing comparison as pending. |
| DM-002 | ASSUMPTION — The pilot uses identifiable, reusable crates throughout the trip, and each crate makes only one loaded journey per trip. | High — Reused labels or a second load could merge two deliveries. | Review two consecutive manifests and one crate’s issue, return and reuse history. Ask about bags and borrowed crates. | Collection supervisor and driver | Separate the permanent container ID from a unique load ID. Record the container type and issue a new load ID on every loading. |
| DM-003 | ASSUMPTION — Loaded crates can be traced when leaf is split, combined, repacked or partly rejected. | High — A legitimate transfer could appear as missing leaf. | Trace one repacked or rejected load against the original ticket and receiving note. Hold the comparison open when any portion is unaccounted for. |
| DM-004 | ASSUMPTION — A verified empty weight is available for each crate in its weighing condition, including any liner, lid or retained water. | High — A tare error changes the reported net weight at both ends. | Request a video of staff weighing the same empty crate as used at each site. Compare the result with the recorded tare. | Weighing employee and factory receiver | Save the tare, its source and verification time with each reading. Flag missing or stale tare and retain the gross reading. |
| DM-005 | ASSUMPTION — Staff can distinguish gross load weight, net leaf weight and the weight used for payment after deductions. | High — Comparing a physical weight to a payable weight could falsely suggest loss. | Ask staff to annotate a redacted pickup ticket and its matching factory receipt, including units, rounding and deductions. | Weighing employee, factory receiver and accounts clerk | Store units, gross, tare, net and commercial deductions separately. | DM-010 | ASSUMPTION — The factory can specify who may request, approve and dispute a correction, including after a trip closes. | High — Unauthorized changes could conceal loss or overwrite the evidence needed for a dispute. | Request the approval procedure and a redacted corrected ticket. | DM-012 | ASSUMPTION — A discrepancy alert triggers a human review before anyone changes a payment or records misconduct. | High — An unverified reading could affect a worker’s pay or disciplinary record. | Ask the manager to walk through a disputed shortage, including who hears the driver’s explanation and releases payment. | Factory manager and accounts supervisor | Keep discrepancies open for review.
